The rules we will use

1. A radius meets a tangent at a right angle

PC is perpendicular to the vertical side, and PD is perpendicular to the horizontal side. At T, the circles’ centres and the touching point lie on one line.

2. Pythagoras and equal legs

The two perpendicular legs of triangle OCP are both r. Its diagonal is found by Pythagoras, and its two other angles are each 45°.

3. Area of a sector

A sector takes the same fraction of a circle’s area as its angle takes of a full turn.

Sector area=θ360°×πR2

Here R means the radius of that sector’s circle.

Part 1 · the inscribed radius

Turn the touching circles into a right triangle

01

Why the small square has side r

The small circle touches the vertical side at C and the horizontal side at D.
Its centre is P, so PC=PD=r.

PC is horizontal and PD is vertical, because each radius is perpendicular to the side it touches.
The corners O, C, P and D therefore form a rectangle.

Opposite sides of that rectangle are equal:

OC=PD=rOD=PC=r

All four sides are r, so OCPD is a square.
In particular, right triangle OCP has two perpendicular legs of length r.

These lengths come from tangency and the equal radii—not from assuming that the drawing looks square.

02

Describe the centre distance in two ways

First description: Pythagoras in triangle OCP.
The hypotenuse is OP, so:

OP2=r2+r2=2r2OP=r2

Second description: the line through the touching circles.
Join O to P, then extend the line to the touching point T.

The large radius OT is 8, given in the question.
The final piece PT is a small-circle radius, so it is r.

OP+PT=OTOP+r=8OP=8−r
Why subtract?
The small circle is inside the large circle. The line is O → P → T, so OP is what remains of 8 after removing PT.

At T, both radii are perpendicular to the same common tangent. That is why O, P and T are collinear.

03

Equate the two descriptions of OP

We have not found two different lengths.
We have two expressions for the same segment OP:

OP=r2OP=8−r

Therefore those expressions must be equal:

r2=8−rr2+r=8r(2+1)=8r=82+1

Multiply top and bottom by 2−1:
the denominator becomes (2+1)(2−1)=2−1=1.

r=8(2−1)r≈3.3137 units

Keep the exact form for the area calculation. Rounding now would make the final area less accurate.

Part 2 · the black shaded region

Take one large sector and remove two pieces

We are not subtracting the whole small circle from the whole quadrant. The question colours only the upper gap.

04

Start with a 45° sector of the large circle

Triangle OCP has equal perpendicular legs, so its two remaining angles share 90° equally:

∠COP=∠CPO=45°

O, C and A lie on one vertical line.
O, P and T lie on the other straight line we drew.
Therefore ∠AOT=45°.

The whole sector AOT uses the large radius 8:

Area of sector AOT=45°360°×π×82=18×64π=8π
This sector includes the black region plus two extra pieces. We must remove those pieces before we have the requested area.
05

First piece to remove: triangle OCP

Look at the blue triangle.
It occupies the bottom of sector AOT, below the horizontal line CP.

Its perpendicular base and height are both r:

Area of triangle OCP=12×r×r=r22

This triangle is not black in the question.
All of it must be removed from the large sector.

You do not need the sloping length OP for this area. Use the perpendicular sides OC and CP.

06

Second piece to remove: sector CPT

Look at the gold piece.
Its centre is P, not O.
It belongs to the small circle, so its radius is r, not 8.

We already know ∠CPO=45° from triangle OCP.
PO and PT point in opposite directions along one straight line.

The two angles at P add to a straight angle:

∠CPO+∠CPT=180°∠CPT=180°−45°=135°

Now take that fraction of the small circle’s area:

Area of sector CPT=135°360°×πr2=38πr2
Where did 135 come from?
It was calculated as 180 − 45. It is not an extra number given in the question.
07

Subtract only the two unwanted pieces

The three pieces exactly fill sector AOT:

Blue triangle OCP

One straight-sided piece at the bottom.

Gold sector CPT

One curved piece belonging to the small circle.

After removing both, the black region is all that remains.

S=8π−r22−38πr2

To substitute the radius, first square the exact result:

r2=8(2−1)2=64(2−22+1)=64(3−22)

Every quantity is now known: 8 comes from the question, r from Part 1, and both sector angles from the equal-leg triangle.

Check another way: why “quadrant minus circle, then divide by two” is not enough

Outside the small circle but inside the quadrant, there are three gaps.

The black upper gap and the right-hand gap are mirror images across OT, so their areas are equal.
But a third, unshaded corner gap remains next to O.

The square OCPD has area r2.
Inside it, a quarter of the small circle has area πr24.

Corner gap=r2−πr24

Subtract the full circle and that corner gap from the quadrant; only then divide the two equal remaining gaps by 2:

S=16π−πr2−(r2−πr24)2=8π−r22−38πr2

It is exactly the same expression as the sector method.
